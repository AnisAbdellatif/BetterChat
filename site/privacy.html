Privacy Policy

BetterChat · last updated 28 September 2026

This policy covers the BetterChat website at betterchat.tech and the “KickPlus” browser extension. Both are run by one independent developer and are not connected to Kick.

BetterChat has no accounts, no cookies, no advertising and no trackers. It does not collect personal information, and it does not send your chat activity anywhere. The one thing it can send is an anonymous count of how many tabs are watching, and only if you say yes when asked. The extension’s live chart, while you have it switched on, also asks KickTracker for the figures of the channel on screen (below).

Changed on 28 September 2026: the extension’s live chart now reads its figures from KickTracker by default, instead of only gathering them in the Kick tab. See The live chart and KickTracker.

What BetterChat stores

Your settings — font, colours, filters, which badges to show and so on — are saved by your browser in localStorage, on your own device. They are never sent to us. Clearing your browser’s site data removes them.

The extension stores five settings: enabled, whether chat replacement is on; useDev, whether to load the test build instead of the normal one; showViewers, whether the live chart is on; viewersRange, how many minutes that chart shows; and chartSource, whether the chart reads its figures from KickTracker or gathers them in the Kick tab. They are kept in chrome.storage.sync, so Chrome may sync them between devices you are signed in to. They hold no personal information.

The live chart and KickTracker. The chart is off until you switch it on. While it is on, by default, the extension asks KickTracker (kicktracker.anisabdellatif.me), a statistics site run by the same developer, for the figures of the channel on screen: once a minute, the last 15 minutes of its viewer counts, active chatters and hosts. Each request names that channel and carries a key built into the extension, the same for everyone. It carries nothing about you: no account, no id, nothing from your Kick session. Your IP address arrives with it, as with any web request; KickTracker holds it in memory for about a minute to limit request rates, and does not store it. Its server’s operational log notes each request’s path and time — that is, which channel was asked about — never who asked. KickTracker only records channels it already tracks, and asking about one does not start tracking it. Like BetterChat, it is reached through Cloudflare, which processes requests in transit under its own terms.

Switch Chart data from KickTracker off in the extension’s popup and the extension never contacts KickTracker. The same happens for any channel KickTracker does not track: the chart then gathers its figures in the Kick tab, as follows. It asks Kick for the current channel’s viewer count every 15 seconds, from the kick.com tab, the way kick.com’s own page loads channel details. To count active chatters it also listens to that channel’s public chat feed, the same one kick.com’s chat uses, and notes only each sender’s Kick user id and when they last wrote; message text is not kept, and entries older than 15 minutes are discarded. These readings are held in your browser’s memory (chrome.storage.session), so the chart survives reloading the Kick tab, and are never sent to us. They are erased when you switch the chart off or quit the browser.

Where the extension gives moderators delete, timeout and ban buttons, it reads your Kick login token from your browser only to make that one request to Kick, as you, the way kick.com’s own page does. The token is read per action, is never stored, and never goes anywhere but Kick.

What BetterChat sends to us

From the extension, only the live chart’s requests to KickTracker described above, and only while the chart is on and set to read from KickTracker.

From the BetterChat page, nothing at all, unless you agree to the viewer count. You are asked once, on your first visit, and nothing is sent before you answer. Declining is a normal answer and the chat works exactly the same either way. You can change your mind at any time under Overlay & sharing in the settings.

If you agree, the page sends a short message when it joins a channel, every five minutes while it is open, and once as it closes. Each one contains:

  • a random id for this browser tab, made up on the spot and forgotten when the tab closes — it is not tied to you, your device or your other tabs;
  • the channel being watched;
  • how many chat messages went by since the last message we sent;
  • whether the page is embedded in kick.com or opened directly;
  • whether you are on the normal site or the test build.

That is used for one thing: showing how many people are watching, and how busy the channels are, on a board only the operator can see. There is no profile, no history tied to you, and none of it is shared with anyone or used for advertising. Nothing about your messages, your Kick account or your identity is included, because the page never has any of it to send.

Like any web request, ours arrive with your IP address attached. It is used to answer the request and is not stored with the count.

What your browser contacts directly

BetterChat shows Kick chat by talking to Kick from your own browser, exactly as a kick.com tab does. Your browser connects directly to Kick to:

  • look up the channel and its recent messages;
  • hold a live WebSocket connection to Kick’s chat feed;
  • load emote images, badges and profile pictures.

Those requests go to Kick, not through us. Kick receives them as it would any other visit to their service, including your IP address, and its handling of them is covered by Kick’s own privacy policy.

With the extension’s chart on and reading from KickTracker, the extension also contacts kicktracker.anisabdellatif.me, as described above.

Server logs

Requests for the BetterChat page and its files reach a small web server, which keeps the ordinary operational logs every web server produces: IP address, time, requested path and user agent. They exist to keep the service running and to diagnose faults. They are not combined with anything else, not used to profile anyone, and not shared or sold. Traffic reaches that server through Cloudflare, which acts as our network provider and processes requests in transit under its own terms.

Sharing

We do not sell, rent or share personal data. There is none to share.

Children

BetterChat is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone.
